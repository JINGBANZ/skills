# X Writer

## Overview

Most posts get scrolled past because they ask the reader to invest attention with no visible payoff. A post earns engagement when the reader can see — in the first line — what's in it for them, and the body delivers it.

**Core principle:** Lead with a concrete, specific payoff. Then deliver real value in the reader's preferred format. Stay in the user's authentic voice.

This skill is for *writing the post*. It does not run accounts, schedule, or automate posting.

## When to Use

- User asks to draft a tweet, thread, or X article
- User has a draft that "isn't landing" / "got no views" / "feels boring"
- User wants help turning knowledge (a lesson, result, how-to) into a post
- Critiquing a post before it ships

**When NOT to use:** non-X long-form (blog posts, newsletters, LinkedIn have different norms), or pure growth-ops questions (scheduling, analytics, automation).

## The Hook Is Everything

The first line is an ad for the rest of the post. If it doesn't promise a payoff, nothing below it gets read. Write the hook last if you must, but never ship without a deliberate one.

**Strong hooks share traits:**
- A concrete number or result — `$10k this month`, `8% install-to-paid`, `3 ways to…`
- A clear, narrow benefit the reader wants
- Curiosity gap: enough to intrigue, not enough to satisfy

| Weak hook | Why it fails | Stronger rewrite |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| "Some thoughts on app marketing" | No payoff, no specificity | "How I get 8% install-to-paid (most apps get 2%)" |
| "I've been working hard lately" | Nothing in it for the reader | "I made $4.7K from one channel in 90 days. Here's the playbook." |
| "Paywalls are important" | Obvious, no tension | "I A/B tested 6 paywalls. The winner doubled revenue. What changed:" |

Apply the hook test to every draft: **read only line one — would a stranger keep reading?** If not, rewrite it.

## Provide Real Value

Engagement is a trade: the reader gives attention, the post must give value back. Value = something the reader can *use* — a lesson, a result with the mechanism, a how-to, a contrarian take backed by evidence.

- Anchor to the user's niche/skill, not generic platitudes.
- Show the *how* and the *number*, not just the claim. "I optimized my paywall" → "I moved the trial CTA above the fold and conversion went 3% → 6%."
- Memes and stray thoughts with no hook, story, or result get scrolled. If you write one, know it's volume filler, not a value post.

## Match the Format

X rewards reposting the same knowledge in different shapes. When drafting, pick the format that fits the content:

| Content shape | Best format |
|---------------|-------------|
| One sharp insight or result | Single tweet |
| Step-by-step / multi-point lesson | Thread (1 idea per tweet, each tweet self-contained) |
| Deep how-to with sections | X article (use a hook title) |
| Showing a process, demo, or result | Video — raw beats polished; X pushes video hard |

Threads: the first tweet is the hook and must stand alone; end with a TL;DR or CTA. Don't bury the payoff in tweet 4.

## Stay in the User's Voice

Authenticity outperforms a polished persona, especially as AI-generated sameness floods the feed.

- Write how the *user* actually talks — match their register (lowercase/casual vs. formal), their slang, their level of bluntness. Ask for or mirror samples of their past posts.
- Let them show failures and honest opinions, not just wins. Real specifics > safe generalities.
- Don't sand a draft into generic "LinkedIn-voice." If it reads like every other thought-leader post, it's wrong.

## Drafting Workflow

1. **Find the payoff.** What concrete value/result does the user have? Get the number or specific outcome.
2. **Write the hook.** Lead with that payoff. Apply the hook test.
3. **Deliver in the body.** Show the mechanism — the how behind the what.
4. **Pick the format** that fits the content (single / thread / article / video script).
5. **Voice pass.** Rewrite in the user's authentic register; strip generic filler.
6. **Cut.** Remove any line that doesn't add value or momentum.

## Common Mistakes

- **Burying the hook** — the best line sits in the middle. Move it to line one.
- **Claim without mechanism** — "grew 10x" with no how. Readers want the lever you pulled.
- **Vague abstractions** — "provide value," "be consistent." Replace with the specific thing you did.
- **Over-polishing voice** — turning a punchy casual draft into corporate mush.
- **No CTA on a value post** — when appropriate, tell the reader what to do next (follow for more, reply, read the thread).

## Quick Reference

- Hook first, payoff visible in line one.
- Numbers and specifics beat adjectives.
- One idea per tweet; threads' first tweet stands alone.
- Show the how, not just the what.
- Raw video > polished video.
- Sound like the user, not like AI.
